I care about the truth

AI has drowned the Internet in low-quality content

AI enables the creation of meaningless content with no original thought or research. There’s been an explosion of AI-generated web pages over the last few years, making it dramatically more difficult to find useful and accurate information online.

  • Futurism: Over 50 Percent of the Internet Is Now AI Slop, New Data Finds

AI constantly generates harmful misinformation

AI has no means of distinguishing between truth and falsehood, and constantly says things that are subtly or even blatantly incorrect.

  • The Guardian: Google AI Overviews put people at risk of harm with misleading health advice
  • AP: Researchers say an AI-powered transcription tool used in hospitals invents things no one ever said
  • IEEE: Is AI Search a Medical Misinformation Disaster?
  • BBC: Largest study of its kind shows AI assistants misrepresent news content 45% of the time – regardless of language or territory
  • AP: Cats on the moon? Google’s AI tool is producing misleading responses that have experts worried
  • Arxiv: Search engines post-ChatGPT: How generative artificial intelligence could make search less reliable
  • Blog: “AI”: A Dedicated Fact-Failing Machine, or, Yet Another Reason Not to Trust It For Anything
  • Site: Welcome to the Vibe Graveyard

AI obscures primary sources of information

AI chatbots and search summaries have replaced traditional research for many people, obscuring primary sources and reducing the quality of information they find.

  • TechRadar: Goodbye Google? People are increasingly switching to the likes of ChatGPT, according to major survey – here’s why
  • HNN: AI Is an Anti-Primary Source Machine

AI is eroding trust in our institutions

Use of AI is exacerbating the public’s declining trust in essential institutions, from governments to civil courts.

  • Rest of World: Five times AI hallucinations embarrassed governments
  • BU School of Law: How AI Destroys Institutions
  • Reuters: US appeals court orders lawyer to pay $2,500 over AI hallucinations in brief

Claims about AI’s abilities are highly exaggerated

AI’s effect on global productivity has been far more limited than advertised. Additionally, companies in multiple industries have repeatedly been caught making false, exaggerated, or unverifiable claims about the abilities of AI products and features.

  • SSRN: Large Language Models, Small Labor Market Effects
  • Economic Policy: The simple macroeconomics of AI
  • The AI Counsel: FTC Evaluating Deceptive Artificial Intelligence Claims

  • Science Daily: Concerns over ’exaggerated’ study claims of AI outperforming doctors
  • BBC: ‘I’m being paid to fix issues caused by AI’

Executives and employees alike feel pressured to speak positively about AI because doing otherwise would harm their careers.

  • TechRadar: Workers feel pressure to use AI and show off to bosses — even when it slows them down
  • Business Insider: There’s a new job-interview dilemma: How excited about AI should you sound?
  • New Republic: How AI Has Created a Braggy Culture of Layoffs

I care about the economy

AI companies are financed by deceptive circular deals

Circular financing allows AI companies to claim high valuations and revenue forecasts without actually making money. For example: Nvidia invests $100M in OpenAI and OpenAI signs a deal promising to spend that $100M on AI hardware from Nvidia; Nvidia then claims $100M of expected revenue and OpenAI claims a $100M valuation on the shares it sold, without either of them creating any real market value.

  • The Conversation: Who’s paying whom? The circular money machine behind the AI boom (or bubble)
  • Gizmodo: Nvidia Tries to Defend Against Circular Dealmaking Accusations
  • CNBC: Jim Cramer warns AI’s circular financing frenzy echoes the dot-com bubble
  • Blog: The Case Against Generative AI

AI has created a speculative bubble, risking economic disaster

AI spending and valuations have reached unrealistic highs, with many experts forecasting a crash that could mirror the dot-com bubble.

  • The Register: AI bubble could burst and take global economy with it, IMF warns
  • The American Prospect: The AI Bubble Is Bigger Than You Think
  • Investopedia: AI Now Accounts for a Third of US Market Value—What That Means For The Economy
  • CBS: The AI bubble is leaking air, some economists say. Should investors worry?

AI is an incredibly inefficient technology

Large Language Models scale poorly, getting slower and requiring more resources with each model size increase. The “economy of scale” does not appear to apply here.

  • The Atlantic: Generative AI Is an Engineering Disaster
  • Epoch AI: Training compute costs are doubling every eight months for the largest AI models

AI has driven up prices for consumer electronics

AI development requires massive amounts of computer memory. The race to sign multi-year contracts and buy up inventory from chipmakers has caused electronics prices to rise, negatively affecting non-AI companies and everyday consumers.

  • Forbes: How AI Is Driving Up The Costs Of Phones, Games And Computers
  • Popular Mechanics: The AI Boom is Making Tech More Expensive: What Shoppers Should Expect in 2026

AI is destroying the financial model of digital publishing

Digital media and news sites have lost a significant amount of web traffic since the advent of AI chatbots, causing serious financial difficulties.

  • The Guardian: Publishers fear AI search summaries and chatbots mean ‘end of traffic era’
  • The New American: Internet Traffic to Top News Sites Has Collapsed 33 Percent Globally Due to AI
  • Blog: The Internet’s Most-Read Tech Publications Have Lost 58% of Their Google Traffic Since 2024

AI claims to make users faster and more productive, but often fails to live up to this promise.

  • Harvard Business Review: AI-Generated “Workslop” Is Destroying Productivity
  • The Register: AI coding tools make developers slower but they think they’re faster, study finds
